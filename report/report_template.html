Cress Farming

Building a cress combine harvester with an N20-motor cutter bar — design, step-by-step build guide, safety plan and upgrade manual.

Companion to the interactive presentation at brendanjameslynskey.github.io/Cress_Farming ·

Summary for parents

What it is. A small working model harvester, about 24 cm long, built mostly from foam board, lolly sticks, bamboo skewers and bottle caps. It rolls along two wooden rails beside a shallow tray of home-grown cress. A paddle reel sweeps the cress onto a plastic cutter bar, which snips it with a scissor action, and the cut cress goes up a short ramp into a small tank.

How the cutter works. A 6 V, 300 RPM “N20” gear motor — a standard hobby motor about the size of a sugar cube — turns a small crank. A lolly-stick arm turns that rotation into a 10 mm back-and-forth slide, moving a plastic knife over a fixed plastic guard comb. That makes ten gentle scissor cuts a second. It is the same principle as the knife on a real combine harvester.

Safety at a glance

6 V AA batteries (no shock risk); plastic knife; self-resetting fuse; ON/OFF switch; hold-to-run button; batteries removed before touching the cutter; an adult does all craft-knife and hot-glue work.

Cost and time

About £20 if household items are used (about £28 all new). Two afternoons of building; the cress grows for a week in parallel. Everything fits in a shoebox.

What gets learned

Plant biology, measuring, ratios and rates, mechanisms (cranks and gearboxes), simple circuits and safety devices, and methodical testing and fault-finding.

A design, not a tested kit

The dimensions in this report are careful starting points. Chapter 19 (Troubleshooting) lists what to adjust if something doesn’t work first time, which is a normal and valuable part of an engineering project.

Parts checklist

  • N20 gear motor, 6 V, 300 RPM
  • 4 × AA battery box with switch
  • 4 AA batteries
  • Momentary push button (normally open)
  • Resettable PTC fuse, 500 mA
  • 100 nF ceramic capacitor (“104”)
  • Lever connectors and hook-up wire
  • A3 foam board, 5 mm
  • A4 stiff plastic, 1 mm
  • 2 × wooden strips 10 × 10 × 450 mm
  • 6 jumbo lolly sticks
  • 4 bamboo skewers + narrow straws
  • 6 brass split pins
  • 4 matching bottle caps
  • Rubber bands, cable ties, masking tape
  • Shallow tray (≤ 110 mm wide) and a small tub
  • Cress seeds and kitchen paper
  • Board for the field, candle for wax

Pre-run sign-off (tick every item before each harvest)

  • Knife slides freely; no cracked or bent teeth
  • Crank pin has its stop glued on the tip
  • Split pins on the arms and knife are secure
  • Wires tied clear of the crank, belt and wheels
  • Releasing the button stops the knife at once
  • End-stop switch stops both motors (if fitted)
  • Machine runs straight on the rails with no crop
  • Tray on its board, towel underneath
  • Hands washed; tray clean; cress will be rinsed
  • Everyone knows: batteries out before touching the knife
